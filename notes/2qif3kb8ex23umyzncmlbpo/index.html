Energy method approximation of small Laplacian deformations on Sobolev spaces

The energy method is a very natural way to see the bound

∣∣u∣∣Xk≤C∣∣f∣∣Xk,(−εΔ+1)u=f||u||_{X^k} \leq C ||f||_{X^k}, \qquad (-\varepsilon \Delta + 1) u = f∣∣u∣∣Xk​≤C∣∣f∣∣Xk​,(−εΔ+1)u=f

and in this problem it runs very cleanly because the Laplacian commutes with itself so you can differentiate the equation by powers of Δ\DeltaΔ without producing nasty commutators. Below I give a self-contained energy-style proof that

∥u∥H2s≤∥f∥H2s(and hence ∥u∥Hk≤Ck∥f∥Hk for all k),\|u\|_{H^{2s}} \le \|f\|_{H^{2s}}\qquad(\text{and hence } \|u\|_{H^{k}}\le C_k\|f\|_{H^{k}}\text{ for all }k),∥u∥H2s​≤∥f∥H2s​(and hence ∥u∥Hk​≤Ck​∥f∥Hk​ for all k),

and then show how to combine that with the L∞L^\inftyL∞-estimate to get the XkX^kXk-bound. Everything below holds on a compact Riemannian manifold SSS (no boundary), and the constant is independent of ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0.

1. The basic L2L^2L2 energy estimate

Start from

(−εΔ+1)u=f.(-\varepsilon\Delta + 1)u = f.(−εΔ+1)u=f.

Take the L2L^2L2-inner product of the equation with uuu. Using ⟨−Δu,u⟩=⟨∇u,∇u⟩=∥∇u∥L22\langle -\Delta u,u\rangle = \langle\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle = \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2⟨−Δu,u⟩=⟨∇u,∇u⟩=∥∇u∥L22​​ (integration by parts; no boundary terms on a compact manifold) we obtain

ε∥∇u∥L22+∥u∥L22  =  ⟨f,u⟩≤∥f∥L2∥u∥L2.\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2 + \|u\|_{L^2}^2 \;=\; \langle f,u\rangle \le \|f\|_{L^2}\|u\|_{L^2}.ε∥∇u∥L22​+∥u∥L22​=⟨f,u⟩≤∥f∥L2​∥u∥L2​.

From this coercivity we immediately get

∥u∥L2≤∥f∥L2,\|u\|_{L^2} \le \|f\|_{L^2},∥u∥L2​≤∥f∥L2​,

and also ε∥∇u∥L22≤∥f∥L2∥u∥L2≤∥f∥L22\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2 \le \|f\|_{L^2}\|u\|_{L^2} \le \|f\|_{L^2}^2ε∥∇u∥L22​≤∥f∥L2​∥u∥L2​≤∥f∥L22​, but we will not need the latter for the main uniform-in-ε\varepsilonε HkH^kHk-bounds. The important point: the operator is coercive and gives an L2L^2L2-control of uuu by fff with constant 1.

2. Higher (even) derivatives by applying powers of Δ\DeltaΔ

Because Δ\DeltaΔ commutes with itself and with the scalar 1, we can apply Δs\Delta^{s}Δs (for any integer s≥0s\ge0s≥0) to the PDE and get

(−εΔ+1)(Δsu)  =  Δsf,(-\varepsilon\Delta + 1)\bigl(\Delta^{s} u\bigr) \;=\; \Delta^{s} f,(−εΔ+1)(Δsu)=Δsf,

(i.e. Δs\Delta^{s}Δs passes through the constant-coefficient operator −εΔ+1-\varepsilon\Delta+1−εΔ+1). Now take the L2L^2L2-inner product of this equation with Δsu\Delta^{s}uΔsu. Exactly the same coercivity algebra gives

ε∥∇(Δsu)∥L22+∥Δsu∥L22=⟨Δsf,  Δsu⟩≤∥Δsf∥L2 ∥Δsu∥L2.\varepsilon\|\nabla(\Delta^{s}u)\|_{L^2}^2 + \|\Delta^{s}u\|_{L^2}^2 = \langle \Delta^{s} f,\;\Delta^{s}u\rangle \le \|\Delta^{s}f\|_{L^2}\,\|\Delta^{s}u\|_{L^2}.ε∥∇(Δsu)∥L22​+∥Δsu∥L22​=⟨Δsf,Δsu⟩≤∥Δsf∥L2​∥Δsu∥L2​.

Therefore

∥Δsu∥L2≤∥Δsf∥L2.\|\Delta^{s}u\|_{L^2} \le \|\Delta^{s}f\|_{L^2}.∥Δsu∥L2​≤∥Δsf∥L2​.

This is the same structure as the base L2L^2L2 inequality, but at the differentiated level — and again the constant is 1 and independent of ε\varepsilonε.

3. From ∥Δs(⋅)∥L2\|\Delta^{s}(\cdot)\|_{L^2}∥Δs(⋅)∥L2​​ to Sobolev norms

On a compact manifold the Sobolev norm H2sH^{2s}H2s is equivalent to the graph norm of the (positive) Laplacian:

∥w∥H2s≃(∑j=02s∥∇jw∥L22)1/2\|w\|_{H^{2s}} \simeq \Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\|\nabla^j w\|_{L^2}^2\Bigr)^{1/2}∥w∥H2s​≃(∑j=02s​∥∇jw∥L22​)1/2

and, spectrally, ∥w∥H2s\|w\|_{H^{2s}}∥w∥H2s​​ is equivalent to ∥(1−Δ)sw∥L2\| (1-\Delta)^s w\|_{L^2}∥(1−Δ)sw∥L2​​. In particular there are constants C1C_1C1​, C2C_2C2​​ (depending only on sss and the geometry of SSS) such that

C1∥Δsw∥L2≤∥w∥H2s≤C2(∥w∥L2+∥Δsw∥L2).C_1\|\Delta^{s} w\|_{L^2} \le \|w\|_{H^{2s}} \le C_2\bigl(\|w\|_{L^2}+\|\Delta^{s} w\|_{L^2}\bigr).C1​∥Δsw∥L2​≤∥w∥H2s​≤C2​(∥w∥L2​+∥Δsw∥L2​).

Using the estimate ∥Δsu∥L2≤∥Δsf∥L2\|\Delta^{s}u\|_{L^2}\le\|\Delta^{s}f\|_{L^2}∥Δsu∥L2​≤∥Δsf∥L2​ together with the base L2L^2L2-bound ∥u∥L2≤∥f∥L2\|u\|_{L^2}\le\|f\|_{L^2}∥u∥L2​≤∥f∥L2​​ gives

∥u∥H2s≤Cs ∥f∥H2s\|u\|_{H^{2s}} \le C_s\,\|f\|_{H^{2s}}∥u∥H2s​≤Cs​∥f∥H2s​

for some constant CsC_sCs​​ depending only on sss and the manifold SSS, but not on ε\varepsilonε. (If you use the spectral identity ∥w∥H2s≃∥(1−Δ)sw∥L2\|w\|_{H^{2s}}\simeq\|(1-\Delta)^s w\|_{L^2}∥w∥H2s​≃∥(1−Δ)sw∥L2​ you can even arrange Cs=1C_s=1Cs​=1 for the part coming from Δs\Delta^sΔs; the equivalence constants appear when moving between different definitions of the Sobolev norm.)

This proves uniform-in-ε\varepsilonε control for all even Sobolev orders H2sH^{2s}H2s.

4. Odd / arbitrary integer k

If kkk is arbitrary integer, pick sss with 2s≥k2s\ge k2s≥k. The control in H2sH^{2s}H2s and the continuous embedding H2s↪HkH^{2s}\hookrightarrow H^{k}H2s↪Hk (on compact SSS) yield

∥u∥Hk≤Ck∥f∥Hk.\|u\|_{H^{k}} \le C_{k}\|f\|_{H^{k}}.∥u∥Hk​≤Ck​∥f∥Hk​.

So every integer Sobolev order is handled. For non-integer orders use interpolation (or fractional powers of 1−Δ1-\Delta1−Δ); the same argument with (1−Δ)σ(1-\Delta)^{\sigma}(1−Δ)σ in place of Δs\Delta^sΔs (or spectral functional calculus) gives the estimate for all real k≥0k\ge0k≥0.

5. The L∞L^\inftyL∞-part and the XkX^kXk-estimate

If the manifold has dimension nnn and k>n/2k>n/2k>n/2, Sobolev embedding gives Hk↪L∞H^k\hookrightarrow L^\inftyHk↪L∞. From step 3 we have a uniform HkH^kHk-bound

∥u∥Hk≤Ck∥f∥Hk.\|u\|_{H^{k}} \le C_k \|f\|_{H^{k}}.∥u∥Hk​≤Ck​∥f∥Hk​.

Therefore also

∥u∥L∞≤Cemb∥u∥Hk≤Ck′∥f∥Hk.\|u\|_{L^\infty} \le C_{\mathrm{emb}} \|u\|_{H^k} \le C'_{k}\|f\|_{H^k}.∥u∥L∞​≤Cemb​∥u∥Hk​≤Ck′​∥f∥Hk​.

Combining ∥u∥Hk\|u\|_{H^k}∥u∥Hk​​ and ∥u∥L∞\|u\|_{L^\infty}∥u∥L∞​​ gives the desired XkX^kXk-bound

∥u∥Xk=∥u∥Hk+∥u∥L∞≤Ck′′ ∥f∥Xk.\|u\|_{X^{k}} = \|u\|_{H^k} + \|u\|_{L^\infty} \le C_k'' \,\|f\|_{X^{k}}.∥u∥Xk​=∥u∥Hk​+∥u∥L∞​≤Ck′′​∥f∥Xk​.

(If the XkX^kXk-norm is defined as the max or another equivalent norm, the same conclusion holds with possibly different constants.)

6. Comments / why this is cleaner than a naive energy attempt

  • The key simplification is that you may apply powers of Δ\DeltaΔ directly to the equation because Δ\DeltaΔ commutes with −εΔ+1-\varepsilon\Delta+1−εΔ+1. That avoids any commutator terms which would otherwise introduce ε−1\varepsilon^{-1}ε−1-type factors or geometric curvature terms.
  • The estimate is uniform in ε\varepsilonε because the factor ε\varepsilonε multiplies the −Δ-\Delta−Δ term on the left-hand side but every differential-level energy identity keeps the same coercive shape: ε∥∇(Δsu)∥L22+∥Δsu∥L22=⟨Δsf,Δsu⟩,\varepsilon\|\nabla(\Delta^s u)\|_{L^2}^2+\|\Delta^s u\|_{L^2}^2=\langle\Delta^s f,\Delta^s u\rangle,ε∥∇(Δsu)∥L22​+∥Δsu∥L22​=⟨Δsf,Δsu⟩, and the inequality ∥Δsu∥L2≤∥Δsf∥L2\|\Delta^s u\|_{L^2}\le\|\Delta^s f\|_{L^2}∥Δsu∥L2​≤∥Δsf∥L2​ follows exactly as in the base case without producing ε−1\varepsilon^{-1}ε−1 factors.
  • If you had variable coefficients in front of Δ\DeltaΔ (e.g. −ε∇⋅(A(x)∇u)-\varepsilon \nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla u)−ε∇⋅(A(x)∇u)) or lower-regularity metric terms, commutators would appear and you would need to control them; that can introduce dependence on ε\varepsilonε unless handled carefully.
