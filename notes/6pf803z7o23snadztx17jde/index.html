Remarks / why Ricciardi mentioned Gε→δG_\varepsilon\to\deltaGε​→δ

  • The convergence Gε⇀δG_\varepsilon\rightharpoonup\deltaGε​⇀δ explains the limiting behaviour of the solution as ε↓0\varepsilon\downarrow0ε↓0: u=(Gε∗f)→fu=(G_\varepsilon * f)\to fu=(Gε​∗f)→f pointwise (and in suitable norms) — consistent with the fact that (−εΔ+1)−1→Id(-\varepsilon\Delta+1)^{-1}\to\mathrm{Id}(−εΔ+1)−1→Id.
  • But to prove the operator norm bound ∥u∥Xk≤Ck∥f∥Xk\|u\|_{X^k}\le C_k\|f\|_{X^k}∥u∥Xk​≤Ck​∥f∥Xk​​ you do not actually need the weak convergence; you only need the two facts used above:
    1. Gε(⋅,y)≥0G_\varepsilon(\cdot,y)\ge0Gε​(⋅,y)≥0 and ∫SGε(⋅,y)=1\int_S G_\varepsilon(\cdot,y)=1∫S​Gε​(⋅,y)=1 for each yyy (gives the L∞L^\inftyL∞ bound),
    2. the spectral multiplier (1+ελ)−1(1+\varepsilon\lambda)^{-1}(1+ελ)−1 has modulus ≤1\le1≤1 (gives the HkH^kHk bound).

So the convergence to the delta is a helpful intuition and gives pointwise limit information, but the inequality itself follows from the kernel mass + positivity and from the spectral (Fourier multiplier) estimate.
